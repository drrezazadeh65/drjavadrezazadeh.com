Editorial approach

What distinguishes an evidence-informed insight from a promotional update?

An educational article should begin with a meaningful question, clarify the relevant concepts, identify the kinds of evidence that can address the question and explain what remains uncertain. For example, a discussion of talent identification should distinguish current achievement from developmental potential, and a discussion of assessment should distinguish the reliability of a score from the validity of the interpretation made from it. Readers should leave with a clearer way to reason about the problem, not merely a new term to search for.

Time-sensitive announcements require a different standard: dates, roles, publication status and institutional associations should be verifiable and updated when circumstances change. Conceptual commentary should not be presented as empirical findings, and emerging projects should not be described as independently validated unless appropriate evidence exists. These editorial boundaries help maintain continuity between public communication and academic responsibility.

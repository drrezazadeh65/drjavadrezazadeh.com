A contextual approach to teacher professional learning

Professional learning is more useful when it begins with a situated pedagogical question. An issue involving student participation, feedback, learning materials or classroom assessment cannot always be understood without considering the particular learners, institutional expectations and constraints under which the teacher works. The proposed engagement therefore centres on articulating one manageable problem rather than prescribing a universal teaching method.

Evidence from an appropriately anonymised lesson account, learning material or reflective record may support a provisional explanation of the difficulty. A development plan can then identify actions worth trying and indicators for later reflection. Such indicators help the teacher exercise professional judgement; they do not constitute a guaranteed measure of improved teaching effectiveness.

For teachers, teacher educators or programme leaders requiring more than one isolated consultation to reflect on teaching and assessment practice.

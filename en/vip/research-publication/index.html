From manuscript diagnosis to a responsible publication decision

A publication strategy begins with the specific contribution a manuscript can substantiate, rather than with a promise of acceptance or a list of supposedly easy journals. A diagnostic reading can examine the coherence of the research question, the relationship between claims and evidence, the role of previous scholarship and the extent to which the argument addresses a recognisable readership. Revision priorities should distinguish substantial conceptual difficulties from matters of presentation.

Journal fit requires examination of the journal’s current aims, article types, peer-review arrangements and author guidance, alongside any relevant ethical or methodological requirements. The outcome of this service is a reasoned set of revision priorities and publication options; it is not peer review by a journal, editorial endorsement, ghostwriting or guaranteed publication.

International Collaboration

Collaboration begins with a clear scholarly purpose.

This page is for legitimate academic and professional collaboration: research dialogue, scholarly publishing, invited talks, teacher education, conference activity and carefully scoped educational projects. It is not a public window into confidential or unreleased research.

Possible forms of collaboration

Where collaboration may be useful

Research Dialogue

Conceptual discussion, literature-informed collaboration and research design around Applied Linguistics, education, assessment, materials and learner development.

Academic Publishing

Book chapters, edited work, peer-reviewed publication, scholarly reviewing and publishing conversations where roles and standards are explicit.

Teacher Education

Professional learning, materials literacy, assessment literacy and research-informed teacher development.

Invited Talks & Webinars

Talks, panels and discussion-based events in areas directly connected to verified academic and professional work.

Educational Projects

Carefully scoped public-facing educational initiatives when the purpose, audience, evidence base and responsibilities are clear.

Scholarly Networks

Conference, journal and professional-association activity that strengthens legitimate academic exchange rather than manufactured visibility.

Working principles

Clarity before commitment

Defined scope

What is being proposed, for whom, and with what scholarly or educational purpose?

Verifiable roles

Authorship, contribution, reviewing, speaking and institutional claims should reflect real participation.

Protection of unreleased work

Confidential manuscripts and unpublished frameworks are not disclosed through public collaboration pages.

Responsible communication

Initial enquiries should contain enough context to assess relevance without sending sensitive documents or private student data.

Invite Dr. Rezazadeh

Speaking and institutional work should begin with purpose, audience and expected contribution.

Invitations are considered where the proposed role connects to verified academic or professional work and where the organiser can define the audience, format and practical purpose. The pathway is intentionally broader than keynote speaking because the most useful contribution may instead be a workshop, panel, teacher-development session, research dialogue or scoped institutional consultation.

Professional rationale

For researchers with a defined question about design, methods, interpretation or argumentation. Effective professional engagement begins with a precise problem formulation and the careful identification of evidence rather than an assumption that any general package can resolve every situation.

The quality of a research decision depends on more than selecting a technique. The consultation examines the fit among the research question, design assumptions, data, permissible inferences and relevant ethical conditions. It can clarify choices but cannot manufacture evidence or replace the investigator's responsibility.

Deliverables and necessary qualifications

A 60-minute analysis of the research issue and the next decision. The work is shaped by the information actually available, the professional responsibilities of the client and any limits that must accompany interpretation.

Limits of this service: The adviser does not generate data or undertake the research for the client.

Work sequence

  1. Define the intended decision or professional problem.
  2. Confirm the information available and the actual scope.
  3. Undertake the agreed review, consultation or structured work.
  4. Communicate the output, its limitations and relevant next steps.

Academic evidence and limits of interpretation

The choice of a method should follow the research question and its evidential requirements rather than the popularity of a particular statistical procedure. Clear reporting of assumptions, methods and limitations helps readers evaluate the warrant of an inference, although a bounded consultation cannot substitute for the researcher’s ongoing investigation.

A researcher's guide to this professional service

A researcher faces a specific question about design, validity, qualitative interpretation, quantitative evidence or responsible inference.

What to prepare

Identify the research question, stage of work, methodological decision and constraints. There is no need to share a complete dissertation in an open contact form.

Scope and scholarly responsibilities

A 60-minute discussion evaluates competing methodological options and what evidence would justify them. It does not replace the researcher's responsibility to conduct and report the study.

Illustrative example, not a real client case

Illustrative case: evidence from one classroom is presented as representative of all learners. Consultation can clarify the relationship between context, sampling and generalizability.

Professional rationale

For a school or organisation needing to define an educational problem and an initial course of action. Effective professional engagement begins with a precise problem formulation and the careful identification of evidence rather than an assumption that any general package can resolve every situation.

Institutional problems require explicit attention to stakeholders, constraints and intended measures of progress. Before promising a programme intervention, the consultation should distinguish the presenting concern from the underlying organizational conditions and agree what evidence could justify a next step.

Academic evidence and limits of interpretation

An educational institution should distinguish the visible presenting problem from constraints relating to staff, leadership, learners and available resources. Research on professional learning can inform intervention design, but transferring a programme between contexts without local evidence can undermine its practical value.

Illustrative organisational diagnosis: the problem behind participation

A school describes low learner participation as a motivation problem. The first task is to distinguish that interpretation from the evidence available.

  1. Specify when and for whom participation has changed, rather than treating the entire school as one undifferentiated group.
  2. Consider how timetabling, lesson design, assessment and feedback could interact with learner experience.
  3. Identify low-risk options for investigation, without prematurely committing the institution to a major intervention.
  4. Define a measurable observation that would inform the next decision.

A 90-minute diagnostic discussion is not an institutional audit, implementation contract or longitudinal evaluation. Those activities require a separately agreed scope.

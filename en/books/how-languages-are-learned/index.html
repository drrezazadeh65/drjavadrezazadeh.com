In Press · Persian scholarly edition

How Languages Are Learned

A Persian translation, scholarly rereading and independent comparative extension focusing on children's Persian-language development and acquisition.

This project is based on the fifth edition (2021) of How Languages Are Learned by Patsy M. Lightbown and Nina Spada. The independently authored Persian comparative extension relates the original scholarship to questions of childhood language development while keeping source authorship and new interpretations clearly distinct.

International research and the Persian linguistic context

How Languages Are Learned offers a research-informed account of language learning. The Persian project seeks to make the original scholarship accessible to Persian-language readers while critically considering its relevance to local developmental and educational contexts. However, evidence about second-language learning cannot necessarily be transferred without qualification to first-language development. The comparative sections therefore distinguish their respective populations, methods and explanatory assumptions.

Why an independent comparative extension?

The translation is separated from independently authored scholarly analysis. The supplementary discussion of Persian language acquisition and development, more recent research and methodological questions is not attributed to Lightbown and Spada. This separation supports scholarly transparency and allows readers to evaluate both the source work and the additional arguments on their own terms.

The extension also considers the value of longitudinal and context-sensitive research involving the spontaneous speech of Persian-speaking children, including questions of developmental emergence, frequency, productivity and contextually appropriate use.

Readership

The project is intended for scholars and students of applied linguistics, language acquisition and language education, childhood-language researchers, teachers and interested Persian-speaking readers.

Teacher Education

Professional learning should strengthen judgement, not simply add techniques.

Teacher development is treated here as sustained professional learning: understanding learners, selecting and adapting materials, interpreting assessment evidence, engaging critically with research and using technology without surrendering professional responsibility.

Professional learning

Five areas that should remain connected

Pedagogical Judgement

Moving beyond technique accumulation toward decisions that respond to learner evidence, purpose and context.

Materials Literacy

Understanding what materials assume, enable and constrain, and when principled adaptation is educationally necessary.

Assessment Literacy

Reading scores and other evidence responsibly, recognising uncertainty and avoiding decisions that claim more than the evidence supports.

Research Engagement

Using research as a resource for professional reasoning rather than as a set of decontextualised prescriptions.

Technology & AI Responsibility

Using educational technology in ways that preserve learner agency, teacher responsibility and the visibility of genuine learning.

Professional learning in practice

From professional development events to sustained classroom inquiry

A workshop may introduce a useful idea, but participation alone does not demonstrate a change in teaching or student learning. Suppose a group of teachers seeks to improve written feedback. A manageable professional-learning cycle might begin with the examination of anonymised examples, proceed to the trial of one agreed feedback strategy, and conclude with a review of learners’ subsequent work and teachers’ implementation experiences. The purpose is not to attribute every observed change to a single intervention, but to make the grounds for the next professional decision more visible.

Such work requires space for teachers to exercise judgement rather than simply comply with a prescribed technique. Professional learning is strengthened when colleagues can question assumptions, recognise contextual constraints and explain why a particular adaptation is warranted. Evidence from practice is indispensable, yet its interpretation should remain proportionate to the design and quality of the inquiry.

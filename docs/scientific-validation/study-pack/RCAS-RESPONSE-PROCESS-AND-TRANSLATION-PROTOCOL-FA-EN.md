# RCAS Start · response-process and translation study pack
**Revision:** 2026-10-16-R1 · **Status:** RESEARCH DESIGN ONLY — NOT ETHICALLY APPROVED, RECRUITING OR COLLECTING DATA.

## Intended unit and scientific question
Compare the existing **28-prompt, no-score** FA (`start-1.2`) and EN (`start-en-pilot-1`) browser activities for comprehensibility, interpretation, emotional burden, cultural appropriateness, and usability of the self-selected seven-day action. This is **not** a factor-analysis or norming study. The English edition is a *translation pilot*, not a validated cross-lingual form.

**Locked references before recruitment:** exact item list and UI/translation content digest for both editions; participant-facing descriptions; locale, participant population and recruitment mode; approved consent/assent; dates; research team; data retention/deletion policy; ethics determination; preregistration and independent translation panel signoff.

## Recruitment and population (study team to preregister)
Begin with consenting adult learners/educators for low-risk cognitive interviews; no minors until independent legal/ethics/guardian/assent architecture is approved. Purposively sample heterogeneous language proficiency, education, accessibility needs and familiarity with reflective questionnaires. Record sampling rationale and recruitment flow; do not imply that any fixed number constitutes statistical validation. Determine a targeted qualitative sample in advance and define information-power/stopping rationale, with explicit documentation of uncovered subgroups.

“You are being invited to discuss how understandable and appropriate the wording of a learning-reflection activity is. This is a study of questions and user experience, not an evaluation of your talent or competence. Participation is voluntary; you may skip any question or stop. No diagnostic or individual score will be provided. Recording, access, retention, researcher contact details and withdrawal procedures must be specified in a separately approved information sheet before enrolment.”

## Interview procedure (each participant)
1. Confirm approved consent, adult eligibility, voluntary choice, language preference, accommodations; use participant code rather than name in analysis.
2. Introduce the tool as *reflection*, not measurement or diagnosis. Ask what the participant expects to happen before showing items.
3. Ask the participant to navigate the current interface with assistive technologies where relevant; silently observe stumbling blocks and request permission before notes/audio.
4. Purposefully sample each of the 28 prompts across the study, ensuring every prompt is independently checked by multiple participants; avoid exhausting a single participant. Do not reveal an “intended right response.”
5. Use neutral probes: “What did this statement mean to you?”; “Which situation were you thinking of?”; “How did you choose this answer?”; “Was any word unclear, sensitive or culturally awkward?”; “Would you interpret it differently in another language?”
6. Ask participant to explain how the seven-day action is selected and revised. Note expectations around privacy and server storage.
7. Explicitly ask about offensive framing, exclusion, pressure to self-label, cultural and disability access barriers, and perceived negative effects. Stop or refer according to approved safeguarding plan if distress emerges.
8. End with a teach-back: participant explains what the activity **can** and **cannot** infer. Offer withdrawal instructions and study team contact from the approved sheet.

## Coding and controlled outputs
Use a preregistered codebook with `ITEM_ID, VERSION, LOCALE, COMPREHENSION, AMBIGUITY, CULTURAL_CONTEXT, BURDEN, ACCESSIBILITY, RESPONSE_REASONING, REQUESTED_REVISION, RESOLUTION`. Example categorical coding for *analysis*, not participant score: `clear | minor_difficulty | major_misinterpretation | concerning_content`; document coding rubric, independent double coding and disagreements. Only de-identified synthesis may enter peer review. Preserve dissent and negative cases.

Do not calculate internal consistency, factor structure, DIF or normative percentages from this study. If quantitative scores are proposed later, prepare a separately powered/precision-justified protocol and validation dossier, not an opportunistic reuse of cognitive-interview notes.

## Independent translation/decision record
For each prompt record FA authoritative wording, EN candidate, translator expertise and independence, reconciliation rationale, independent target-language review, cognitive-interview finding, accessibility adaptation, both edition hashes and acceptance/hold/revise decision. Treat linguistic equivalence as a **hypothesis** until empirical evidence supports it.

## Stop/go
**GO (before recruitment):** independent ethics approval; legitimate documented consent, staff training and limited secure analysis storage; all 28 prompts mapped; translation reviewers identified; withdrawal and distress response documented.  
**GO (after study):** report the full error taxonomy, revisions, limitations and negative cases.  
**NO-GO:** any claim of 28-item score validity, equivalent FA/EN scale, psychometric norms, diagnosis or release of private learner data.

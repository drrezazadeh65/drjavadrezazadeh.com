Peer Review

A review process designed to be rigorous, proportionate and accountable.

The journal is being designed around double-anonymised external peer review for research articles, with editorial screening before review and independent handling where conflicts arise.

Planned workflow

Editorial screening will consider scope, scholarly contribution, ethical adequacy, methodological fit and basic reporting quality. Manuscripts proceeding to review will ordinarily be evaluated by at least two qualified reviewers. Decisions will distinguish clearly among reject, major revision, minor revision and accept; acceptance will not be based on author status, institutional prestige or presumed citation potential.

Review quality

Reviewers will be asked to evaluate the coherence between research questions, theory, method, evidence and claims. The journal will discourage requests for unnecessary citation, coercive citation, methodological gatekeeping unsupported by the research question, or demands that authors make claims broader than their evidence warrants.

Reviewer responsibilities and independence

The planned double-anonymised process will require reviewers to identify potential conflicts before accepting an assignment, preserve the confidentiality of manuscripts and refrain from exploiting privileged research information. Editorial screening will precede external review; reviewers will be chosen for demonstrated disciplinary and methodological competence rather than affiliation, personal proximity or institutional prestige.

Constructive review should make the basis of a recommendation intelligible. It should distinguish between limitations that affect the validity of a conclusion and improvements that concern clarity of presentation or a justified alternative theoretical position. Requests for additional analysis or new data must be proportionate to the research question and the evidence already available.

Decisions, appeals and research integrity

The handling editor will consider the reviewers' reasoning and the manuscript's contribution, rather than treating reviewer votes as an automatic decision rule. Disagreements may warrant additional specialist assessment. Decisions involving conflicts of interest, including any manuscript authored by the founding Editor-in-Chief, must be delegated to an independent editor with no role for the conflicted person in reviewer selection or acceptance.

A documented procedure for appeals, complaints, potential research-integrity concerns and post-publication correction must be tested before formal submissions open. These commitments describe the intended editorial model; they are not a claim that completed peer-review cases already exist.

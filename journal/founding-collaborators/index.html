We invite senior and emerging academics who are interested in helping shape an independent international journal through editorial, methodological and scholarly collaboration.

Areas of interest

Education and learning; educational assessment and evaluation; applied linguistics and language education; curriculum and materials; teacher education and professional learning; educational technology and AI; higher education; talent development; mixed-methods research; educational guidance; fairness, accessibility and ethics.

Who should apply?

Expressions of interest are welcome from faculty members, experienced researchers and scholarly publishing professionals who can contribute to editorial governance, methodological quality, international networking or the founding issue.

What to send

Please email: full name; academic rank; institution and country; institutional email where available; ORCID; research areas; methodological expertise; recent CV; selected publications; and a brief statement explaining why you would like to serve as a founding editorial collaborator. Applications are reviewed before any public appointment is made.

No automatic appointment

Submitting an expression of interest does not create an editorial title, ownership right or employment relationship. Reviewer, board and founding roles are confirmed separately after scholarly and conflict-of-interest review.

Founding editorial collaboration requires clarity of roles and safeguards

The early development of an academic journal presents methodological and organisational questions that cannot be resolved through the reputation of individual contributors alone. Editorial roles, decision authority, reviewer selection, conflicts of interest and publication ethics must be explicit. Establishing these conditions is particularly important in fields that bring together different educational traditions and empirical approaches.

Potential collaborators should be able to understand the initiative's proposed scope, current stage and the responsibilities of any role before committing. An invitation to contribute ideas or professional experience is not evidence that a formal appointment has already taken place. Transparent expectations support both institutional accountability and the independence of scholarly evaluation.

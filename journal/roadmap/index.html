Scholarly development roadmap · Reviewed 16 October 2026

From responsible journal formation to international scholarly influence

A scholarly journal becomes credible through the cumulative quality of published research, independent editorial judgement, accountable governance and consistent service to a research community. JHELA is making that trajectory public, while distinguishing development commitments from verified achievements. Several stages may advance in parallel, and indexing outcomes remain subject to independent external evaluation.

Present status and claims — Current as of 16 October 2026: JHELA remains at founding stage; no formal manuscript intake, registered ISSN or DOI, first published issue or indexing has been independently certified. This roadmap is not a ranking guarantee.

What would count as genuine progress?

A manuscript counted without independent review, an unregistered DOI, publication without a preservation plan, or an index logo without an official listing would offer no credible evidence of maturity. Each milestone therefore distinguishes policy design, source implementation, production testing and decisions of independent scholarly organisations.

DOAJ ordinarily requires at least five research articles annually, with newly launched journals additionally needing more than one year of publication history or ten genuine open-access research articles before application. Scopus removed its general two-year waiting condition in August 2024, although adequate publication history remains necessary for evaluation.

# 2. Current SERP patterns

## A. Field-selection results
Strong current pages tend to combine:
- clear year-sensitive targeting, especially ۱۴۰۵;
- a service proposition above the fold;
- rank/academic record + interest + personal conditions;
- field and university comparison;
- future/career language;
- multiple service tiers or packages;
- free tools or test entry points;
- long-form informational guides connected to the paid service;
- visible calls to action.

### Risk
Many pages collapse “possible admission”, “personal fit”, “career prospects” and “personality-test output” into one recommendation layer. That creates an opportunity for a more careful evidence model, but we must not merely copy the language of “personalised” and “data-driven” used across the market.

### Our differentiation
Our page should continue to separate:
1. **possible** options;
2. **suitable** options;
3. **preferred** options;
4. **official annual constraints**;
5. **uncertainty and information still needed**.

The public proposition remains: **decision support, not a pre-written answer.**

---

## B. Entrance-exam counselling results
Strong pages commonly emphasise:
- weekly planning;
- continuous follow-up;
- daily/weekly reporting;
- performance analysis;
- one assigned counsellor;
- goal/target definition;
- dashboard or student record;
- clear workflow from intake to follow-up.

### Risk
The market often turns “more monitoring” into a value claim without showing whether the monitoring improves judgement or learning.

### Our differentiation
The roadmap should preserve:
- executable planning rather than maximal study hours;
- monitoring that changes the plan when evidence changes;
- no guaranteed rank;
- separation of educational support from pressure/identity;
- transparent human review;
- eventual private longitudinal student record.

This makes the future portal and CRM part of the service itself, not a cosmetic dashboard.

---

## C. Broad academic counselling results
Current pages often combine:
- entrance exam;
- field selection;
- school-level guidance;
- study planning;
- tests/tools;
- downloadable resources;
- broad “from school to doctorate” coverage.

### Risk
A very broad site can become topically diffuse and create cannibalisation.

### Our decision
The broad `/fa/moshavere-tahsili/` page remains the service hub, while distinct transactional intents are owned by:
- `/fa/entekhab-reshteh/`
- `/fa/moshavere-konkur/`
- `/fa/estedaadyabi/`

Additional service pages are created only when user need and SERP intent are genuinely distinct.

---

## D. Talent-identification space
Visible competitors frequently use:
- personality/interest tests;
- Holland/RIASEC;
- “scientific tests” language;
- AI/data claims;
- career matching;
- free test entry points.

### Risk
The language of scientific certainty is often stronger than the evidence warrants.

### Our differentiation
Golden Talent must remain:
- multi-source;
- developmental;
- contextual;
- versioned;
- explicit about limitations;
- non-deterministic;
- linked to parent/teacher/student evidence;
- capable of human review.

A test result is an input, not a verdict.

# 4. Conversion architecture learned from the SERP

The public service funnel should become:

**Informational article → relevant service page → intake → triage → booking → payment (if required) → consultation → record/report → follow-up**

The future Golden Talent funnel should become:

**Educational resource → free screening → account → versioned assessment → snapshot → optional paid report/human review → Golden Path → follow-up**

The journal funnel remains completely separate:

**Journal information/call → role-specific application → editorial verification → role acceptance → OJS workflow**

---

# 5. What we will NOT copy from competitors

- inflated certainty;
- guaranteed results/ranks;
- opaque “AI says this career is right for you” claims;
- one-test career determinism;
- aggressive countdown/scarcity without a real deadline;
- fake review counts or testimonials;
- city doorway pages with duplicated copy;
- mass-produced “all fields/all cities” pages with thin value;
- product schema before real purchase availability;
- annual data without verified official-source checking.

---

# 6. Design implications

Commercial/service pages need:
- one clear proposition above the fold;
- a visible process;
- a clear “who this is for” section;
- evidence/limitations;
- deliverables;
- FAQ;
- internal links to explanatory articles;
- a low-friction consultation request;
- mobile-first actions;
- later, transparent price/package comparison only when real pricing is approved.

The current consulting pages satisfy the conceptual core but will require stronger operational CTAs once booking/payment infrastructure exists.
